A serious diagnosis brings bills your health plan does not touch: time off work, travel to a treatment center, childcare, and a deductible that resets every January. Critical illness and cancer insurance pay cash directly to you, so you can focus on recovery.

What to compare

  • Covered conditions and definitions. Two policies can both say "heart attack" and define it differently.
  • Waiting periods before benefits start, and pre-existing condition limits
  • Recurrence benefits if a condition comes back
  • Issue rules. Some plans skip health questions in exchange for lower benefit amounts
  • Riders such as wellness screening benefits

These plans pair well with a high-deductible or HSA plan, and they are worth a look for anyone who lost a subsidy for 2027 and moved to a lower-premium plan.

Frequently asked questions

What is critical illness insurance?

A policy that pays a lump sum when you are diagnosed with a covered condition, such as a heart attack, stroke, or cancer. You decide how to spend it.

How is cancer insurance different?

Cancer-only policies focus on cancer and often pay benefits for treatments like chemotherapy and radiation in addition to, or instead of, a diagnosis lump sum.

Does it cover a condition I already have?

Usually not right away. Most policies exclude or limit pre-existing conditions and have a waiting period after the policy starts.

Does my health plan already cover this?

Your health plan pays medical bills. These policies pay cash for everything else: deductibles, travel for treatment, and lost income.

This is supplemental coverage. It does not count as minimum essential coverage under the Affordable Care Act and does not replace a major medical health plan.
