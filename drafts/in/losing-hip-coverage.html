Starting January 1, 2027, most Healthy Indiana Plan (HIP) members must meet new work and activity requirements, and eligibility will be checked every six months instead of once a year. The state estimates more than 58,000 Hoosiers could lose HIP coverage in 2027. If you or someone in your family is on HIP, here is what to know now.

The most important thing first: if you lose HIP because you did not meet or report the work requirement, federal law bars you from a marketplace premium tax credit. Keeping HIP, or claiming an exemption you qualify for, is almost always cheaper than any private plan. Report your hours through the state before you think about anything else.

The new HIP rules

  • Who: HIP members age 19 to 64 who are not pregnant and not eligible for Medicare
  • What: 80 hours a month of work, job training, volunteering, or school, or earnings of at least $580 a month
  • Lookback: three months, so October through December 2026 counts for January 2027 applications
  • Checks: eligibility every six months; hours checked more often
  • Exemptions apply for some caregivers, people with certain health conditions, and others. See the FSSA HIP page.

If you lose HIP anyway

Lost HIP because your income went up

Losing Medicaid is a qualifying event. You generally have 60 days to enroll in a HealthCare.gov plan, and you may qualify for a premium tax credit. We can tell you within minutes.

Lost HIP for not meeting work requirements

You can still buy a marketplace plan during open enrollment (November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027) or after a qualifying event, but without a premium tax credit. Lower-cost options include Bronze plans, short-term coverage where it fits, and job-based coverage if your employer offers it.

Got a job with benefits?

Compare the employer plan to what you would pay on your own. If the employer offers an ICHRA, see our ICHRA guide.

Short-term coverage in Indiana

Indiana law allows short-term plans with initial terms up to 364 days and up to 36 months in total, with at least $2 million in benefits, but each carrier sets its own limits and many sell shorter terms. Short-term plans are not ACA coverage and usually exclude pre-existing conditions. Learn more.

Frequently asked questions

When do the HIP work requirements start?

January 1, 2027. They apply to Healthy Indiana Plan members age 19 to 64 who are not pregnant and not eligible for Medicare, unless an exemption applies.

What do I have to do to keep HIP?

Show 80 hours a month of work, job training, volunteering, or school, or earn at least $580 a month, or qualify for an exemption. Indiana looks back three months, so October through December 2026 counts for January applications.

If I lose HIP, can I get a subsidized marketplace plan?

It depends on why. Under federal law, people who lose Medicaid for not meeting work requirements cannot get a premium tax credit. If you lose HIP for another reason, such as higher income, you may qualify for a special enrollment period and a subsidy.

Is there still a year-round enrollment option for low-income people?

No. The special enrollment period for people under 150% of the poverty level has ended, so timing matters. Losing coverage generally gives you 60 days to enroll in a marketplace plan.
