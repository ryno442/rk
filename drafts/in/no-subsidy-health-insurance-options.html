Ways to lower your cost without a subsidy

1. Re-shop every carrier, on and off HealthCare.gov

Without a subsidy, the exchange price and the carrier's direct price are the same, and some plans are sold only off-exchange. Prices for the same metal level often differ by hundreds of dollars a month between carriers.

2. Use an HSA-qualified Bronze plan

Since 2026, Bronze and catastrophic plans bought through the marketplace count as HSA-qualified. For 2027 you can put up to $4,500 (self-only) or $9,000 (family) into an HSA before federal tax.

3. Check whether your income can drop below the line

The credit is based on modified adjusted gross income. Pre-tax retirement contributions and HSA deposits lower it, and for households just over 400% that can restore a credit. Confirm with your tax professional.

4. If you own a business, look at an ICHRA

An individual coverage HRA lets your company reimburse individual premiums tax-free for you and your employees. Ask us whether it beats paying premiums personally.

Short-term health insurance

Indiana law allows short-term plans with initial terms up to 364 days and up to 36 months in total, with at least $2 million in benefits, but each carrier sets its own limits and many sell shorter terms. Short-term plans are medically underwritten, usually exclude pre-existing conditions, and are not ACA coverage, so they fit healthy people bridging a gap, not people managing ongoing care. Learn more about short-term plans.

Add supplemental coverage to a high deductible

Hospital indemnity, critical illness and cancer, and accident plans pay cash directly to you. Paired with a lower-premium Bronze plan, they can cost less in total than a Gold plan for some families. They do not replace major medical coverage.

Turning 65 soon?

Medicare is not tied to income the way marketplace credits are. If anyone in your household is close to 65, plan the switch now. See our Medicare annual enrollment guide.

Frequently asked questions

Who loses their subsidy in 2027?

Households with income above 400% of the federal poverty level no longer qualify for a premium tax credit, because the enhanced subsidies expired at the end of 2025.

Is a cheaper plan off the exchange the same coverage?

ACA plans sold off-exchange follow the same benefit rules as plans on HealthCare.gov. Without a subsidy there is no price advantage to the exchange, and some carriers offer plans only off-exchange.

Can lowering my income bring the subsidy back?

Possibly. Premium tax credits use modified adjusted gross income, so pre-tax retirement and HSA contributions lower it. Talk to your tax professional before relying on this.

Is hospital indemnity a replacement for health insurance?

No. It pays fixed cash benefits and works alongside a major medical plan, for example to help cover a high deductible.
