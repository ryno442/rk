Most people can only buy a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan without health questions during their first six months on Medicare Part B, or in a few federal guaranteed-issue situations. After that, a new insurer can usually ask health questions and decline you.

A growing number of states give Medigap policyholders a yearly window to switch plans without medical underwriting. Most of these are tied to your birthday, so they are called birthday rules. One state uses your policy's anniversary instead, and a few allow changes year-round.

How birthday rules work

  • You must already have a Medigap policy. The rule lets you switch; it does not let you buy a first policy.
  • Each year a window opens around your birthday. During it, you can apply for a new Medigap plan and the insurer cannot turn you down or charge more for your health.
  • The new plan usually has to have equal or lesser benefits (for example Plan G to Plan G, or Plan G to Plan N). Some states require the same plan letter.
  • Some states let you move to any insurer. Others only let you switch with your current insurer or an affiliated company.
  • Birthday rules apply to Medicare Supplement plans only. They do not apply to Medicare Advantage.

If your state is not listed

Texas, Florida, Pennsylvania and other states not in the tables do not have a yearly switching right. Outside your first six months on Part B and the federal guaranteed-issue situations, a new Medigap insurer can ask health questions. Choose carefully the first time, and talk to us before dropping coverage.

How to use your birthday rule

  1. Put your birthday window on your calendar and contact us about two weeks before it opens.
  2. We compare premiums for the plans you are allowed to move to, from the insurers your state allows.
  3. Apply during the window. Most states require the application, not the start date, to fall inside the window.
  4. Keep your current policy until the new one is approved, then set the old one to end the day the new one starts.

Frequently asked questions

Does moving to another state keep my birthday rule?

No. The rule belongs to the state where you live. If you move, the new state's rules apply.

Can I use the birthday rule to switch from Medicare Advantage to a Medigap plan?

No. Birthday rules are for people who already have a Medigap policy. Leaving Medicare Advantage has its own rules; see the federal guaranteed-issue rights on Medicare.gov.

Will my premium go up if I use the birthday rule?

The insurer cannot charge more because of your health. Your premium is based on the new plan's normal rates for your age and area.
