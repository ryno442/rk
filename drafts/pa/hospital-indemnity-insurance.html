A hospital stay can use up most of a high deductible in a few days. Hospital indemnity insurance pays you a set cash amount when you are admitted, so you are not paying that deductible out of savings. It is one of the most popular add-ons for people on Bronze and HSA plans in Pennsylvania.

How it works

  • You choose a benefit amount, for example a lump sum per admission, a daily amount per day in the hospital, or both
  • When a covered stay happens, you file a claim and the carrier pays you directly
  • The payment does not depend on what your health plan covers or what the hospital charges
  • Many plans offer riders for outpatient surgery, ER visits, or ICU days

Who it fits

  • People on a Bronze or HSA plan with a deductible of several thousand dollars
  • Families who lost their subsidy for 2027 and moved to a lower-premium plan
  • Anyone expecting a planned surgery or delivery (check the plan's waiting period and pregnancy rules first)
  • Self-employed people who would lose income during a hospital stay

Frequently asked questions

What does hospital indemnity insurance pay?

It pays a fixed cash amount when you are admitted to the hospital, and on some plans for each day you stay, regardless of what the hospital bills. The money goes to you, not the hospital.

Can I use the money for anything?

Yes. Benefits are paid to you and can go toward your deductible, rent, groceries or lost income.

Do I need health questions to qualify?

It depends on the plan. Some are guaranteed issue with lower benefits; others ask a few health questions. Most have waiting periods or pre-existing condition limits.

Is this the same as health insurance?

No. It does not meet ACA requirements and does not replace a major medical plan. It is designed to sit alongside one.

This is supplemental coverage. It does not count as minimum essential coverage under the Affordable Care Act and does not replace a major medical health plan.
