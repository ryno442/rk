Every hurricane season raises the same questions from our Florida clients: can I refill my medications early, what if I evacuate out of my network, and what if a storm made me miss a deadline? Here are the answers, before you need them.

Before the storm

  • Refill prescriptions early. When your county is under a hurricane warning or a state of emergency, Florida law requires insurers to cover at least a 30-day refill even if it is too soon. Request it within 30 days of the declaration.
  • Photograph your ID cards (medical, dental, Medicare, Part D) and save your carrier's phone number and app login on your phone.
  • Write down your medications, doses, and prescribing doctors.
  • Keep a list of providers where you might evacuate. If you have a PPO or a Medicare Supplement, check which ones accept your plan.

If you evacuate

  • Medicare Advantage: during a declared disaster, plans must cover care from non-contracted providers at in-network cost sharing and waive referral requirements while the emergency lasts.
  • Medicare Supplement: works with any Medicare provider in the country, wherever you are.
  • ACA and employer plans: emergency care is covered out of network. For non-emergency care, call your carrier first, since many relax network rules after a declaration.
  • Keep every receipt. Out-of-network claims are easier with documentation.

After the storm

  • Check whether your doctors and pharmacy are open; your carrier's app often shows temporary locations
  • Ask about telehealth, which most plans cover
  • If your income or address changed, update your marketplace application so your subsidy stays accurate

Frequently asked questions

Can I get my prescriptions refilled early before a hurricane?

Yes. Florida law requires health insurers and HMOs to waive refill-too-soon limits and pay for at least a 30-day supply when your county is under a hurricane warning, a state of emergency, or an activated emergency operations center. Ask your pharmacy as soon as the warning is issued.

A storm made me miss open enrollment. Can I still enroll?

Possibly. If you live in a FEMA-declared disaster area and the storm kept you from enrolling, you can ask HealthCare.gov for a special enrollment period, generally available for 60 days after the FEMA incident period ends. Call 1-800-318-2596.

Does Medicare have a special enrollment period after a hurricane?

Yes, in some cases. If a FEMA-declared emergency kept you from making a Medicare Advantage or Part D change you were eligible to make, you may qualify for a disaster special enrollment period.

What if I have to evacuate and see an out-of-network doctor?

During a declared disaster, Medicare Advantage plans must let you get covered care at non-contracted providers at in-network cost sharing. Many other insurers relax network and referral rules too. Call the number on your ID card and keep receipts.
