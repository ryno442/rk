Florida has one of the most competitive Medicare Advantage markets in the country, with many $0-premium plans in most counties. That makes the Advantage-or-Supplement decision bigger here than almost anywhere. Here is how the two compare for Floridians, including snowbirds and people under 65.

Florida rules worth knowing

  • Your first Medigap window matters most. In the six months after you are 65 and enrolled in Part B, Medigap insurers must accept you without health questions. After that, they generally can ask them.
  • Under 65 on Medicare? Florida requires Medigap insurers to offer coverage to people under 65 with Medicare because of a disability or end-stage renal disease, with a six-month open enrollment after Part B starts.
  • No annual birthday rule. Some states let you change Medigap plans each year without health questions. Florida does not, so choose carefully the first time.
  • Trial right. If you join Medicare Advantage when you first become eligible, or drop a Medigap plan to try Advantage for the first time, federal rules generally let you go back to a Medigap plan within 12 months without health questions.

Who usually picks which

Medicare Advantage tends to fit

  • Year-round Florida residents happy with a local network
  • People who want the lowest premium and value dental, vision, and hearing extras
  • People in good health who rarely see specialists

A Medicare Supplement tends to fit

  • Snowbirds and frequent travelers
  • People who want specific specialists or cancer centers without network limits
  • Anyone who wants predictable costs and can afford the higher premium

Frequently asked questions

Which is better in Florida, Medicare Advantage or a Medicare Supplement?

It depends on how you use care. Medicare Advantage usually has lower premiums and a network with copays. A Medicare Supplement costs more each month but lets you see any provider that accepts Medicare, nationwide, with little or nothing out of pocket depending on the plan.

Can I switch from Medicare Advantage to a Medigap plan later?

You can apply at any time, but outside a guaranteed-issue situation the insurer can ask health questions and decline you. Florida does not have an annual Medigap birthday rule.

I am under 65 and on Medicare because of a disability. Can I get a Medigap plan in Florida?

Yes. Florida requires Medigap insurers to offer coverage to people under 65 who have Medicare because of a disability or end-stage renal disease, with a six-month open enrollment window after enrolling in Part B. Premiums for under-65 enrollees are often higher.

I split the year between Florida and another state. What should I pick?

Snowbirds often prefer a Medicare Supplement because it works with any Medicare provider in any state. Most Medicare Advantage plans cover only emergency and urgent care outside the service area.
